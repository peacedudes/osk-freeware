# 2026-08-23 — blarslib, and what it unlocked

Branch `release-pass-2026-08-21`. Nothing pushed. Eleven checks green.

## blarslib

rdoggett said yes, minus the Microware headers. In as
`SRC/blarslib` + `DEFS/blarsdefs` + `LIB/blarslib.l` (32 of its 36 objects).
Thirteen of its headers were byte identical to Microware's or near enough and
did NOT ship; `DEFS/blarsdefs` carries one-line forwards of our own instead.
Those forwards must include BY ABSOLUTE PATH — `<sgstat.h>` from inside
`blarsdefs/sgstat.h` finds itself, and cpp opens it until it runs out of paths.

Four objects are out: `stat.c` (wants `sys/types.h` to define `dev_t` as a
struct, and the overlay's is `SRC/COMPAT`'s, which deliberately does not),
`ftime.c`/`makenv.c`/`putenv.c` (`time_t`, hidden behind `#ifndef ctime` in the
SDK's `<time.h>`), `gethostname.c` (a quoted `-D` the OS-9 shell will not
carry).

## What it unlocked

  - **uucp_blars** — all five programs. `uuxqt` also needed `pw_giduid`, OS-9's
    single packed group.user id, which our `pwd.h` did not have and blarslib's
    `getpw.c` never set.
  - **uucpbb** — 21 of 24. Out: `login` (wants an `<os9.h>` nothing here has),
    `chown` and `cnvrtmail` (assign to `fd_own`, an ARRAY in this `<direct.h>` —
    a port written against a version where it was one number, and not something
    to guess at in a program that changes ownership).
  - **smail 2.5** — all four. `SRC/COMPAT/types.h` is new: the SDK's
    `<types.h>` stops at `u_char` and friends, so `off_t message;` in
    `deliver.c` failed as a declaration and reported as `message` undeclared
    forty lines later.
  - **SB-Prolog** — its makefile builds `builtin/` as a library first; naming
    those sources alongside the simulator's comes to the same thing.

## Not unlocked, and why

  - **macutils** — needs `struct stat` as blarslib declares it, and there is no
    way to give two trees a different `<sys/types.h>`: cpp will not search a
    `-V` directory for an include name with a directory in it. This was the
    argument for taking blarslib in the first place, and it did not hold.
  - **gtar** — everything else is in place now, including a generated
    `testpad.h`, but `cpp` bus-errors on `tar.c`. Same defect as `flex`:
    `notes/CPP-MACRO-CRASH.md`. Two victims now, not one.

## Where the build stands

**271 of 274 recipes build clean** (measured 2026-08-23, whole tree, after the
`COMPAT/types.h` change). The three are `ls` (gcc2), `pdraw` (X11) and `pep`
(the EPROM board's own library). Recipes went from 203 at the start of this
pass to 274.

## Two lessons, both paid for twice

**Do not put a fix in `SRC/COMPAT` when one tree needs it.** `smail`'s
`deliver.c` wants `off_t`; adding it to a new `COMPAT/types.h` looked tidier
than a define and broke `less`, which `#define`s `off_t` itself -- so
`typedef long off_t;` became `typedef long long;`. It is `-Doff_t=long` in
smail's recipe now, scoped to the one tree.

**Do not edit anything a build reads while it is running.** The full build
that caught the `less` regression then reported two more failures that were
only the tree changing underneath it. A run measures the state at the moment
each recipe is reached, not at the start.

## Also

`tools/rebuild/tidy.sh` — puts `disk/` back after a build, touching only files
a build could have made. Twice a hand-typed `git checkout -- disk/SRC` took
deliberate source fixes with the object files.

# 2026-08-23, later — the cpp limit, measured; djpeg builds

A second session the same day, picking up `PLAN-next.md` item 1a: the four
programs stopped by Microware's `cpp`.

## The headline: the cpp bug is a 512-character line limit

Not nested macro expansion. **`cpp` edition 37 bus-errors on a source line of
513 characters or more**, and `c68` refuses one of 1023. Both measured by
feeding each tool one-line files of rising length. It explains the three-file
reproduction this collection has been carrying since 2026-08-22 exactly:
`crash5.c` expands to 542 characters and crashes, `ok4.c` to 270 and
`ok5tiny.c` to 222 and both compile. Five levels of nesting was never the
point; 542 was.

`notes/CPP-MACRO-CRASH.md` is rewritten around that. Its filename is now a
misnomer and is kept because six other files point at it.

**A trap inside the trap.** `cpp` does not always crash on an over-long line —
in some shapes it truncates its output and exits with no diagnostic. An
earlier pass over this same question read those silent truncations as passes
and concluded long lines were fine. Distrust a quiet `cpp`; measure the `.m`.

## djpeg builds, and decodes

`disk/SRC/jpeglib` had `cjpeg` and not `djpeg`, because `jdmarker.c` is a
victim of the above. It needed `KNR` **and** `CPP2` at once — ANSI source
whose one long line kills the preprocessor — and the driver could only do one
or the other. It can do both now, ansi2knr first because cccp2's output is no
longer C that ansi2knr could read.

Verified by running it, not by linking it: `cjpeg` compresses a generated
96x64 PPM, `djpeg` decompresses it, and the two ramps come back at a mean
error of 1.1 and 1.5 of 255 while the deliberate high-frequency channel comes
back noisy. That is a JPEG decoder behaving correctly. `sips` on the host
reads the intermediate as a 96x64 JPEG, so `cjpeg`'s half is honest too.

**The tree's own test images cannot be used** and this is worth knowing before
someone tries: `testorig.jpg`, `testimg.jpg` and `testimg.ppm` in
`SRC/jpeglib/JPEG_5A` have **no byte at or above 0x80 anywhere** — 26,574 of
the PPM's 103,639 bytes are `?`. They went through a 7-bit transfer long
before they got here. `djpeg` on `testorig.jpg` says `Not a JPEG file: starts
with 0x3f 0x3f`, which is the file's fault, not the program's.

## Four changes to the build driver

  - **`KNR` and `CPP2` compose.** Either alone still means what it did.
  - **`KNR=a.c,b.c`** translates only the sources named. `ansi2knr` is safe on
    an ANSI tree and NOT on a K&R one: given `wildmat(s, p)` with the
    parameters declared on the following lines it emits
    `wildmat(s, p)  s; p;` and adds bogus `int` declarations. A blanket `KNR`
    left gtar worse than it found it.
  - **A recipe's `-V=<dir>` reaches the GNU preprocessor**, translated to
    `-I`, and goes in ahead of `/h7` and `/dd/DEFS`. Before this a `CPP2`
    recipe's include directories were silently dropped and every header in
    them came back "file not found".
  - **`cpp2_fixup` re-wraps over-long lines**, and only ever cuts at
    whitespace OUTSIDE a string literal. Both of the usual ways to split a
    literal were tried on `c68` and both fail: `"a" "b"` concatenation is
    ANSI, and a backslash-newline inside a literal is spliced in translation
    phase 2 — which for a `.m` file already happened, in the preprocessor
    being replaced. A line with no safe cut is left long, because
    `input line too long` is a diagnostic and a literal broken in half is a
    mystery.

## Four headers into SRC/COMPAT

`sys/ioctl.h` and `grp.h` and `bcopy.h` forward to headers the disk already
has; `sys/sysmacros.h` is the three device-number macros. Each says in its own
header why it exists. All four are new names — nothing that compiles today can
start resolving differently because of them.

Deliberately NOT added: `sys/mtio.h`. There is no tape ioctl here, and every
use of one in gtar is guarded on `MTIOCTOP`, so leaving it undefined compiles
the right code out.

## Three source lines changed, in gtar, and marked

`create.c:to_unix_mode`, `list.c:to_os9_format` and `buffer.c:rmt_jl_open` were
ANSI definitions — all three additions by the OSK porter rather than FSF code
— and are K&R now, each with a two-line comment saying so and why. This is the
`jconfig.h` precedent: a portability fix, made in place, marked where it was
made. `ansi2knr` could not do it without wrecking the rest of the tree.

## gtar: where it actually stands

Not built. But nothing about the preprocessor is in its way any more, and what
is left is named rather than mysterious — `ERROR`, `TRUE`/`FALSE`, `S_IFREG`
and the Unix `errno` family are undeclared. `notes/CPP-MACRO-CRASH.md` has the
full state, including the two hours that prove `DEFS/os9lib` is a dead end for
this toolchain. Its recipe is deliberately NOT in `recipes.psv`: a recipe means
"known to compile".
