# 2026-08-23 — blarslib, and what it unlocked

Branch `release-pass-2026-08-21`. Nothing pushed. Eleven checks green.

## blarslib

rdoggett said yes, minus the Microware headers. In as
`SRC/blarslib` + `DEFS/blarsdefs` + `LIB/blarslib.l` (32 of its 36 objects).
Thirteen of its headers were byte identical to Microware's or near enough and
did NOT ship; `DEFS/blarsdefs` carries one-line forwards of our own instead.
Those forwards must include BY ABSOLUTE PATH — `<sgstat.h>` from inside
`blarsdefs/sgstat.h` finds itself, and cpp opens it until it runs out of paths.

Four objects are out: `stat.c` (wants `sys/types.h` to define `dev_t` as a
struct, and the overlay's is `SRC/COMPAT`'s, which deliberately does not),
`ftime.c`/`makenv.c`/`putenv.c` (`time_t`, hidden behind `#ifndef ctime` in the
SDK's `<time.h>`), `gethostname.c` (a quoted `-D` the OS-9 shell will not
carry).

## What it unlocked

  - **uucp_blars** — all five programs. `uuxqt` also needed `pw_giduid`, OS-9's
    single packed group.user id, which our `pwd.h` did not have and blarslib's
    `getpw.c` never set.
  - **uucpbb** — 21 of 24. Out: `login` (wants an `<os9.h>` nothing here has),
    `chown` and `cnvrtmail` (assign to `fd_own`, an ARRAY in this `<direct.h>` —
    a port written against a version where it was one number, and not something
    to guess at in a program that changes ownership).
  - **smail 2.5** — all four. `SRC/COMPAT/types.h` is new: the SDK's
    `<types.h>` stops at `u_char` and friends, so `off_t message;` in
    `deliver.c` failed as a declaration and reported as `message` undeclared
    forty lines later.
  - **SB-Prolog** — its makefile builds `builtin/` as a library first; naming
    those sources alongside the simulator's comes to the same thing.

## Not unlocked, and why

  - **macutils** — needs `struct stat` as blarslib declares it, and there is no
    way to give two trees a different `<sys/types.h>`: cpp will not search a
    `-V` directory for an include name with a directory in it. This was the
    argument for taking blarslib in the first place, and it did not hold.
  - **gtar** — everything else is in place now, including a generated
    `testpad.h`, but `cpp` bus-errors on `tar.c`. Same defect as `flex`:
    `notes/CPP-MACRO-CRASH.md`. Two victims now, not one.

## Where the build stands

**271 of 274 recipes build clean** (measured 2026-08-23, whole tree, after the
`COMPAT/types.h` change). The three are `ls` (gcc2), `pdraw` (X11) and `pep`
(the EPROM board's own library). Recipes went from 203 at the start of this
pass to 274.

## Two lessons, both paid for twice

**Do not put a fix in `SRC/COMPAT` when one tree needs it.** `smail`'s
`deliver.c` wants `off_t`; adding it to a new `COMPAT/types.h` looked tidier
than a define and broke `less`, which `#define`s `off_t` itself -- so
`typedef long off_t;` became `typedef long long;`. It is `-Doff_t=long` in
smail's recipe now, scoped to the one tree.

**Do not edit anything a build reads while it is running.** The full build
that caught the `less` regression then reported two more failures that were
only the tree changing underneath it. A run measures the state at the moment
each recipe is reached, not at the start.

## Also

`tools/rebuild/tidy.sh` — puts `disk/` back after a build, touching only files
a build could have made. Twice a hand-typed `git checkout -- disk/SRC` took
deliberate source fixes with the object files.

# 2026-08-23, later — the cpp limit, measured; djpeg builds

A second session the same day, picking up `PLAN-next.md` item 1a: the four
programs stopped by Microware's `cpp`.

## The headline: the cpp bug is a 512-character line limit

Not nested macro expansion. **`cpp` edition 37 bus-errors on a source line of
513 characters or more**, and `c68` refuses one of 1023. Both measured by
feeding each tool one-line files of rising length. It explains the three-file
reproduction this collection has been carrying since 2026-08-22 exactly:
`crash5.c` expands to 542 characters and crashes, `ok4.c` to 270 and
`ok5tiny.c` to 222 and both compile. Five levels of nesting was never the
point; 542 was.

`notes/CPP-MACRO-CRASH.md` is rewritten around that. Its filename is now a
misnomer and is kept because six other files point at it.

**A trap inside the trap.** `cpp` does not always crash on an over-long line —
in some shapes it truncates its output and exits with no diagnostic. An
earlier pass over this same question read those silent truncations as passes
and concluded long lines were fine. Distrust a quiet `cpp`; measure the `.m`.

## djpeg builds, and decodes

`disk/SRC/jpeglib` had `cjpeg` and not `djpeg`, because `jdmarker.c` is a
victim of the above. It needed `KNR` **and** `CPP2` at once — ANSI source
whose one long line kills the preprocessor — and the driver could only do one
or the other. It can do both now, ansi2knr first because cccp2's output is no
longer C that ansi2knr could read.

Verified by running it, not by linking it: `cjpeg` compresses a generated
96x64 PPM, `djpeg` decompresses it, and the two ramps come back at a mean
error of 1.1 and 1.5 of 255 while the deliberate high-frequency channel comes
back noisy. That is a JPEG decoder behaving correctly. `sips` on the host
reads the intermediate as a 96x64 JPEG, so `cjpeg`'s half is honest too.

**The tree's own test images cannot be used** and this is worth knowing before
someone tries: `testorig.jpg`, `testimg.jpg` and `testimg.ppm` in
`SRC/jpeglib/JPEG_5A` have **no byte at or above 0x80 anywhere** — 26,574 of
the PPM's 103,639 bytes are `?`. They went through a 7-bit transfer long
before they got here. `djpeg` on `testorig.jpg` says `Not a JPEG file: starts
with 0x3f 0x3f`, which is the file's fault, not the program's.

## Four changes to the build driver

  - **`KNR` and `CPP2` compose.** Either alone still means what it did.
  - **`KNR=a.c,b.c`** translates only the sources named. `ansi2knr` is safe on
    an ANSI tree and NOT on a K&R one: given `wildmat(s, p)` with the
    parameters declared on the following lines it emits
    `wildmat(s, p)  s; p;` and adds bogus `int` declarations. A blanket `KNR`
    left gtar worse than it found it.
  - **A recipe's `-V=<dir>` reaches the GNU preprocessor**, translated to
    `-I`, and goes in ahead of `/h7` and `/dd/DEFS`. Before this a `CPP2`
    recipe's include directories were silently dropped and every header in
    them came back "file not found".
  - **`cpp2_fixup` re-wraps over-long lines**, and only ever cuts at
    whitespace OUTSIDE a string literal. Both of the usual ways to split a
    literal were tried on `c68` and both fail: `"a" "b"` concatenation is
    ANSI, and a backslash-newline inside a literal is spliced in translation
    phase 2 — which for a `.m` file already happened, in the preprocessor
    being replaced. A line with no safe cut is left long, because
    `input line too long` is a diagnostic and a literal broken in half is a
    mystery.

## Four headers into SRC/COMPAT

`sys/ioctl.h` and `grp.h` and `bcopy.h` forward to headers the disk already
has; `sys/sysmacros.h` is the three device-number macros. Each says in its own
header why it exists. All four are new names — nothing that compiles today can
start resolving differently because of them.

Deliberately NOT added: `sys/mtio.h`. There is no tape ioctl here, and every
use of one in gtar is guarded on `MTIOCTOP`, so leaving it undefined compiles
the right code out.

## Three source lines changed, in gtar, and marked

`create.c:to_unix_mode`, `list.c:to_os9_format` and `buffer.c:rmt_jl_open` were
ANSI definitions — all three additions by the OSK porter rather than FSF code
— and are K&R now, each with a two-line comment saying so and why. This is the
`jconfig.h` precedent: a portability fix, made in place, marked where it was
made. `ansi2knr` could not do it without wrecking the rest of the tree.

## gtar: where it actually stands

Not built. But nothing about the preprocessor is in its way any more, and what
is left is named rather than mysterious — `ERROR`, `TRUE`/`FALSE`, `S_IFREG`
and the Unix `errno` family are undeclared. `notes/CPP-MACRO-CRASH.md` has the
full state, including the two hours that prove `DEFS/os9lib` is a dead end for
this toolchain. Its recipe is deliberately NOT in `recipes.psv`: a recipe means
"known to compile".

# 2026-08-23, overnight — inform, and a second compiler bug

rdoggett went to sleep having said to keep going. This is what got done
unattended.

## inform builds, and it is exact

`inform` -- Graham Nelson's Inform 1.0 (v796), the Z-machine compiler that
produced everything in `GAMES/INFORM` -- has never been built here.
`DOC/INDEX` has carried "this SDK's cpp aborts partway through" for months.

It now builds, and the proof is not that it links: **it recompiles the
collection's own `hellow.inf` to a story file byte-identical to the
`GAMES/INFORM/hellow.z3` that has shipped since day one.**

Three blockers, none of them what the notes said:

  1. **cpp's 512-character line.** informosk.c has one logical line of 775
     characters. `CPP2` clears it.
  2. **`SRC/COMPAT/limits.h` was unusable in a `#if`.** It derived every limit
     through a cast -- `#define INT_MAX _S_MAX(int)`, which expands to
     `((Signed int)((unsigned int) ~0 >> 1))`. That is correct C and always
     zero in preprocessor arithmetic, which has neither casts nor types. ANSI
     requires these macros to work in `#if` for exactly this reason. inform
     selects its 32-bit integer type with a four-arm `#if` over `SCHAR_MAX`,
     `SHRT_MAX`, `INT_MAX` and `LONG_MAX`; every arm was false and it reached

         #error No type large enough to support 32-bit integers.

     The header is plain constants now, MEASURED on this toolchain rather than
     assumed: char 1 byte and signed, short 2, int 4, long 4, pointer 4.
  3. **`o68` miscompiles what `c68 -k` emits** -- below.

## The o68 bug

100K of code cannot reach all of itself with the 68000's 16-bit BSR
displacement, so `r68` says `**** error - branch out of range ****` once per
call. `c68 -k` (force long PC-relative references) is the answer -- and then
`o68`, the object-code improver, silently corrupts string literals.

A ten-case probe, everything built with `-k`, once with o68 and once without:

| construct | with o68 | without |
|---|---|---|
| plain literal | ok | ok |
| **`?:`, TRUE arm** | **wrong** | ok |
| `?:`, FALSE arm | ok | ok |
| **assigned from `?:`, TRUE arm** | **wrong** | ok |
| assigned from `?:`, FALSE arm | ok | ok |
| array of literals | ok | ok |
| function returning one | ok | ok |
| `switch` | ok | ok |
| `if`/`else` | ok | ok |

So it is exactly one construct: the true arm of a conditional expression
choosing between two string LITERALS gets an address eight bytes past the
right one. `one ? "Error" : "Warning"` prints `rning`.

That is what inform was doing. It reported two impossible diagnostics against
every source it compiled -- "Names are not permitted to start with an _"
against a `for` loop -- and finished `Compiled with 2 errors and 0
warningsput)`. Both messages come through `?:` on literals.

**`LONGREF`** is the recipe flag: `c68 -k`, and no `o68` pass. The two cannot
be separated, so they are one word. Dropping o68 costs some optimisation and
nothing else; `r68` assembles c68's output directly. On the ordinary `cc` path
the equivalent is `-K=0CL`, and it needs `-O` for the same reason.

## The strchr(s,0) grep — done, and the guidance was too broad

`FOR-RDOGGETT.md` has said since 2026-08-21 that *any* `strchr(s, 0)` here is
a latent bus error. Measured:

    strchr(s, 0)   unix.l   -> end-of-string   CORRECT
    index(s, 0)    clib.l   -> NULL            wrong
    strrchr(s, 0)  unix.l   -> NULL            wrong
    rindex(s, 0)   clib.l   -> NULL            wrong

`clib.l` has no `strchr` at all, which is why 27 trees here `#define strchr
index`; a `-qm` build takes `strchr` from `unix.l`, and that one is right. So
the idiom is fatal only where the tree ALSO macros it onto `index`.

Whole-tree grep: seven call sites. Six are pdksh's, which does macro it, and
**all six are already covered** by `sh_eval.c.patch`, `sh_history.c.patch` and
`sh_lex.c.patch`. The seventh is `tess/parser.c`, which does not macro it and
so gets the correct function. Nothing to do. Item closed.

## gtar — stopped deliberately, and now fully characterised

Everything blamed on the preprocessor is cleared and committed: `CPP2` plus the
line re-wrap gets all nineteen sources through `cpp` and `c68`, `-DUSG
-DNO_REMOTE -DVARARGS_MSG` are the right switches, four headers went into
`SRC/COMPAT`, and its three ANSI definitions are K&R.

What is left is a PORT DECISION, not a header hunt. The complete undeclared
list, read off the preprocessed output:

    errno EBADF ENOSPC EIO ENXIO EPIPE ENOENT EEXIST
    S_IFREG S_IFCHR S_ISUID S_ISGID S_ISVTX
    TRUE FALSE  /  ERROR  /  time_t, struct timeb
    char *malloc() declaration mismatch

`S_IFREG` is the wall. OS-9 has no regular/character/block distinction and no
setuid bit -- the SDK's `<modes.h>` has `S_IFDIR` and permission bits and
nothing else. The collection's own `blarsdefs/sys/stat.h` answers this
honestly with `#define S_IFREG 0` and `#define S_IFCHR 0`, which then makes
gtar's `switch` a duplicate-case error. What those cases should DO on RBF is
for somebody to decide on purpose.

Ruled out, so nobody repeats it: `DEFS/os9lib` has every missing name and is
ANSI-era (prototypes c68 will not read, a `struct stat` and `dev_t` that
collide with COMPAT's, a `time.h` including `</h0/defs/setsys.h>` by absolute
path); `DEFS/blarsdefs` has neither the Unix errno codes nor usable file-type
bits.

## ed builds — and it was never an ANSI tree

`COMPILE-AUDIT.md` has listed `ed` under "ANSI C" since the category was
written. **It is K&R throughout**, and running `ansi2knr` over it is actively
destructive: `get_sbuf_line (lp)` comes out as `get_sbuf_line (lp)  lp;`. The
audit is corrected.

What actually stopped it was the one thing the README already knew and had
filed under a different heading: `ed.h`'s `REALLOC` macro joins into about
1400 characters, and cpp's limit is 512. `CPP2` clears it, and then the rest
was ordinary work:

  - **`ERANGE`** — the SDK's `<errno.h>` is OS-9's own error numbers and has
    none. `0x100` is the value `DEFS/os9lib/errno.h` gives it, so a caller
    that got it from there and one that got it from the recipe agree.
  - **Three of its own sources were missing from the recipe** —
    `alloca.c`, `xmalloc.c`, `error.c`, all named in its Makefile's `LIBSRCS`.
    The lesson the audit already records: read the tree's own makefile.
  - **`strtol` is in no library a trap-free link can use.** `csl.l` has it and
    needs the trap handler; `ppplib.l` has it and `l68` rejects the file as
    "not a relocatable module". So `shims/os9strtol.c` is new — full ANSI
    behaviour including the end pointer and the ERANGE report, which are the
    two things `atol` cannot do and the only reasons ed wants strtol at all.
  - `os9lib.l` for `umask` and `alloca`, `blarslib.l` for `vfprintf`.
  - **`size_t`.** `xmalloc.c` writes `size_t n;` in a K&R parameter list
    having included only `<sys/types.h>`. `SRC/COMPAT/types.h` supplies it
    now, guarded with `_SIZE_T` — **the SDK's own guard name**, from its
    `<stddef.h>` and `<stdlib.h>`, so whichever header a file reaches first
    the other stands down. `-Dsize_t=unsigned` was tried first and is exactly
    the trap that file's header comment already warns about: it turned
    `typedef long size_t;` into `typedef long unsigned;`.
  - `ed/regex.h` carried an unguarded `#ifdef OSK typedef long size_t;` — the
    porter's line, not the FSF's. It has the `_SIZE_T` guard now, marked.

### A driver bug this found

`ed` linked as **getopt1.c's `#ifdef TEST` self-test**, silently, with no
diagnostic anywhere. The main()-finding loop took the LAST source that matched,
not the first. It takes the first now, which is the source a recipe names
first by convention.

### It wants /r0, and that is the archive's

`buf.c` hardcodes its scratch file as `"/r0/ed.XXXXXX"` — the conventional
OS-9 RAM disk. os9exec has no `/r0`: `OS9R0` is ignored, and `mount -r` from

`disk/CMDS/ed` has the same string. Our build is the better of the two
regardless — it is trap-free, where the shipped one is starred and cannot even
start without `cio`.

## mtools, and the real limit of the KNR flag

`PLAN-next` said mtools "times out at the 30-minute limit, or fails with an
empty log. Worth one careful run." It was run, it finished in minutes, and it
is not a timeout at all: **c68 rejects prototypes on nearly every line**, and
`ansi2knr` did not touch a single definition.

**ansi2knr's own header says why, and it is a CODE STYLE rule, not a header
rule:**

> ansi2knr recognizes functions by seeing a non-keyword identifier **at the
> left margin**, followed by a left parenthesis ... the function name must be
> the first thing on the line.

JPEG writes `METHODDEF(void)` on one line and `start_pass (j_compress_ptr
cinfo)` on the next — name at the margin, so ansi2knr converts it. mtools
writes `static void bad_country_file(void)` all on one line, which begins with
a keyword, so ansi2knr skips it and every prototype reaches c68 intact.

Counting headers whose name is at the left margin against those that start
with a keyword:

    JPEG_5A       348 /  12     ansi2knr reaches it
    MTOOLS_3.6      3 / 477     it does not
    lua/SRC         1 / 236     it does not

That is the correct statement of the KNR flag's limit, and it is sharper than
"the tree's config header has to stop declaring prototypes" (which is also
true, and secondary). **Caution: the count alone does not settle it** — `ed`
scores 118/0 and is not an ANSI tree at all, because a K&R definition has its
name at the left margin too. The count says whether ansi2knr will TOUCH a
tree, not whether it will help.

mtools' OSK port is a `gcc2` build (`CC = gcc2`, `-I/h0/mwos/src/defs/GCC2`),
same as `ls`. That is its real category.

## rayshade builds, and RENDERS — the first package build here

78 sources across six nested libraries. Four separate walls, each measured:

  1. **`<libcommon/common.h>` — a DIRECTORY in the include name.** Microware's
     cpp will not look for such a name in a `-V` directory at all; it goes
     straight to `/dd/DEFS` and fails. This is recorded in `COMPILE-AUDIT.md`
     as the macutils blocker, and it was confirmed here independently.
     **GNU cpp has no such limit**, so `CPP2` dissolves it — every one of
     rayshade's directory-style includes resolved, with no "file not found"
     anywhere.
  2. **`shadow.c`'s stack frame is 39586 bytes**, past the 68000's signed
     16-bit displacement: `*** error - value out of range ***`. `cc -K=2L`
     answers that by switching to `c68020`/`r68020`, and the new `M020` flag
     is the same thing for the CPP2 path.
  3. **And it still overruns**, because a `HitList` is a LOCAL and a HitList is
     `MAXMODELDEPTH` HitNodes. 128 -> 48; 48 assembles and 64 does not, so 48

     frame is per CALL — at 48 with the default 16k stack, `planet.ray`
     rendered three rows and stopped.
  4. `I_STDLIB`, which `funcdefs.h`'s own RCS log names: *"Don't declare free
     or exit void if stdlib.h is used."* Plus one line in `lex.c`, flex's K&R
     fallback `int free();`, which every other declaration on this system
     spells `void`.

**Verified by RENDERING.** `planet.ray` comes out complete at 24x24 and is
recognisably a lit sphere; `solids.ray` and `balls.ray` also complete.
`csg.ray` truncates and `blob.ray` takes a bus error — neither run down, and

## macutils — the audit's reason was right and is now obsolete

`COMPILE-AUDIT.md`: *"blocked on an include-path knot: it needs `struct stat`

## A driver bug this found: same basename, silently clobbered

`binhex` names **both** `BINHEX/binhex.c` (the program) and `CRC/binhex.c`
(its CRC table). The driver derived every temporary from `basename`, so both
became `ctmp_binhex` — the second overwrote the first, and the link then
reported `Symbol 'main' unresolved` with nothing anywhere to say why.

**The first fix was flattening the path** -- `../CRC/binhex.c` ->
`CRC_binhex` -- and it was WRONG, in a way worth recording because it took a
whole-tree build to catch:

    ctmp_libray_LIBCOMMON_expr.raw          31 characters

**An OS-9 filename may be at most 29.** Measured the same day, by creating
names of each length in turn: 29 works, 30 does not. cccp2 could not create
its output, said `file not found` -- about the file it was trying to WRITE --
and the shell's abort-on-error ended the run at the first source. `rayshade`
went from building to not, and `tools/build.sh rayshade` reported it in under
two seconds.

`tmpbase()` suffixes only on an actual collision now: the first `binhex` keeps
that name, the second becomes `binhex_1`. Every other recipe's temporaries are
byte-for-byte what they were, which is the property that makes the change safe
across 288 recipes.

The plain-cc paths cannot do even this, because there `cc -r` names the object
after the source and the collision is cc's rather than ours, so a
same-basename recipe needs `CPP2` for now.

**Two lessons, and the second is the collection's own.** A fix verified on one
recipe is not verified; and the error a tool reports may be about the file it
is WRITING, not the one it is reading.
